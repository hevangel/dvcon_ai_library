# Clock Generation — PLL, DLL, and CDR Verification

> *"A clock is the heartbeat of a chip — but a PLL is the thing that synthesizes that heartbeat out of a slow reference and a phase detector. The trouble is that a PLL is fundamentally analog. It locks by slewing, by dithering, by trading jitter for phase noise, and the only way to know it actually locks in picoseconds is to simulate the closed loop for tens of microseconds of real time. That puts it in the worst spot in the whole verification flow: too slow for digital regressions, too dynamic for a static checker. Cadence's Sreenath and colleagues were already modeling CDR jitter with spread-spectrum clocking in 2014; Wang at Tsing Hua was reporting DCO resolution of 1 ps in 2023; NXP's Chavan was propagating process variations through behavioral PLL models in 2025. So why are we still writing papers about it? Because every node makes the analog harder and every protocol makes the spec tighter. Let's dig in."*

## What it is

**Clock generation** is the discipline of producing the on-chip clocks that sequence every flip-flop, and it rests on three analog workhorses. A **phase-locked loop (PLL)** multiplies a slow reference up to a gigahertz-range clock by comparing phase, filtering, and driving a voltage- or digitally-controlled oscillator. A **delay-locked loop (DLL)** does the same trick without an oscillator, using a delay line to align edges. And **clock-data recovery (CDR)** is the PLL's harder cousin: where a PLL has an explicit reference, a CDR has to reconstruct the clock from the incoming data stream itself, which is why almost every SERDES standard — USB 3.0, PCIe, MPHY, SATA, DisplayPort — needs one [SERDES Rx CDR Verification using Jitter, Spread-spectrum clocking (SSC) stimulus, 2014].

The verification challenge is that all three are **closed-loop analog systems** whose correctness is defined in the time and frequency domains, not in 0/1 logic. The CDR's recovered clock has to track jitter, parts-per-million offset, and spread-spectrum modulation in the incoming data, and its jitter tolerance graph behaves like a low-pass filter with a corner typically in the 5–10 MHz range [SERDES Rx CDR Verification using Jitter, Spread-spectrum clocking (SSC) stimulus, 2014]. Modern implementations split the block: the equalizer, phase interpolator, and samplers are analog, while the CDR algorithm itself runs as digital RTL — so the verification engineer inherits both worlds at once [SERDES Rx CDR Verification using Jitter, Spread-spectrum clocking (SSC) stimulus, 2014]. A cell-based PLL needs a digitally-controlled oscillator (DCO) with sub-picosecond resolution to keep output jitter under control; Wang et al. report a consistently small DCO period profile in the 0.62–1.25 ps range across all five process corners [Design and Verification of the PLL using the new DCO and Its Applications to Built-In Speed Grading of Arithmetic Circuits, 2023].

## How it's used in practice

The dominant pattern is to wrap the PLL/DLL/CDR in a **UVM verification component** that generates, monitors, and checks the clock, so the analog block looks like just another VIP to the digital testbench. Dipanshu et al. at Samsung describe a generic Clock UVC with three agents — a Clock Generator that injects jitter and SSC, a Clock Checker that verifies period, frequency, duty cycle, and phase, and a CDR Agent that extracts the recovered clock and computes the Unit Interval from noisy serial data [A Generic Clock UVC for Generating and Testing of High Speed PLL and CDR, 2024]. The same plug-and-play philosophy shows up in AMD's single UVM platform for verifying multiple PLL IP variants, replacing the older copy-paste-the-testbench approach with one configurable environment [A Unified Testbench Architecture Solution for Verifying Variants of the PLL IP, 2015].

**Jitter modeling** is where most of the engineering effort goes, because jitter is what stress-tests a CDR. Sreenath's team models deterministic jitter with a `1 – cos(x)` function so the excursion is always positive and can be added cleanly to the absolute jitter term; multi-tone jitter is built by summing contributions across tonal frequencies [SERDES Rx CDR Verification using Jitter, Spread-spectrum clocking (SSC) stimulus, 2014]. Chakrabarti and Chikkanna push the same idea further with a parameterized, reusable jitter model that modifies bit width on the link for both serial and parallel interfaces, crucial for stressing CDR recovery when the clock isn't sent alongside the data [Parameterized and Re-usable Jitter Model for Serial and Parallel Interfaces, 2015]. For mixed-level verification, Wang's team runs the PLL datapath at transistor level and the controller in RTL, then layers post-layout simulation on top [Design and Verification of the PLL using the new DCO and Its Applications to Built-In Speed Grading of Arithmetic Circuits, 2023].

## Pitfalls and where the field is heading

The classic pitfall is **regression cost**. CDR jitter tolerance has to be exercised across roughly 100,000 seeds to be confident the loop tracks within bandwidth [SERDES Rx CDR Verification using Jitter, Spread-spectrum clocking (SSC) stimulus, 2014], and that's on top of the underlying analog simulation cost. Emulation helps: Hari et al. move CDC jitter and metastability tolerance onto an emulator to handle multi-billion-gate SoCs with many asynchronous clocks that would choke a simulator [Verification of Clock Domain Crossing Jitter and Metastability Tolerance using Emulation, 2012]. A second pitfall is **treating jitter as a digital afterthought** — Hayek et al. show that a behavioral 2-flop synchronizer model can expose CDC jitter inside any RTL simulation framework, dramatically reducing the risk of finding synchronization problems late in the project [Use of CDC-jitter-modeling in clock-domaincrossing-circuits in RTL design phase, 2017]. Even LPDDR systems, which are both-edge-triggered, can fail on permissible jitter that breaks data sampling — Nagaria et al. describe modeling the clock generators in SystemVerilog to mimic exactly that [Verification strategies and modelling for the uninvited guest in the system: Clock Jitter, 2019].

The field is heading toward **variation-aware behavioral modeling** and **mixed-level flows that scale**. Chavan et al. at NXP construct Variation-Aware Behavioral Models (VABMs) that explicitly preserve inter-block parameter correlations, propagating circuit-level process statistics up to the system level without paying for full Monte Carlo on the whole PLL [Performance Evaluation of a Phase-Locked Loop using Variation-Aware Behavioral Models, 2025]. Viswanathan et al. attack the SPICE runtime problem head-on for digital PLLs by splitting verification into two stages: digital blocks verified with SystemVerilog and analog block models, then a top-level mixed simulation [An Effective Design and Verification Methodology for Digital PLL, 2014]. The long arc is clear: PLLs, DLLs, and CDRs will stay analog at heart, but the verification around them will keep getting more digital, more coverage-driven, and more behavioral — because that's the only way to keep up with the protocols.
