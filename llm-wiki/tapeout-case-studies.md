# Tapeout Case Studies — What Actually Worked

> *"Methodology papers describe how things should go; case-study papers describe how they did go — and the gap is where the real lessons live. There is a sub-genre of DVCon paper that drops the abstractions and tells you, flat-out, what happened on a real chip nearing tape-in: the panic, the late-night formal run that found the starvation bug, the silicon that booted first try because someone verified the RADAR bitfield settings before validation day. Potdar, Kumawat and Srivastava capture the genre's tension perfectly when they note that 'late-stage bug discovery or, worse, post-silicon detection leads to panic and paranoia, making confidence in design quality imperative' — and then describe running last-minute formal verification 'near tape-in date' to claw that confidence back. What unites these papers is that none of them are theoretical. They are post-mortems of shipping chips — Analog Devices interconnect fabrics, Intel graphics SoCs, NXP automotive RADAR, ams-OSRAM sensor systems — written by the people who almost missed their date. The lessons they handed down are concrete, occasionally painful, and unfailingly grounded in what actually worked. Let's read the receipts."

## What it is

A **tapeout case study** is a retrospective grounded in a specific chip, team, and deadline. The unit of analysis is not 'a technique' but 'a project that almost went wrong, and what saved it.' The genre covers the full life cycle: performance modeling, design verification, post-silicon validation, and the bugs that escaped each. Bhatnagar and Fricano's Product Life Cycle of an Interconnect Bus walks through all three phases on one Analog Devices fabric, from SystemC-based performance analysis to verification and post-silicon validation driven by 'a Generic PSS based Traffic Generator' [Product Life Cycle of an Interconnect Bus, 2019]. The point of organizing a paper around a life cycle rather than a technique is that the artifacts — test intent, coverage model, traffic scenarios — have to survive the transition from modeling to silicon, and the case study exposes what actually carried over.

The other recurring frame is **first-pass silicon success**. Tanwar, Kedia and Gupta's HM-model paper is explicitly framed around the SoC-verification problem of 'the absence of RTL design of one or more sub-blocks/hard macros,' whether from unaligned schedules or HM confidentiality, and how building 'an equivalent UVM based model of HM' let a real Qualcomm project verify the SoC anyway [SoC Verification Enablement Using HM Model, 2019]. The case study proves the model was a sound substitute by reporting that silicon came back working — the only verdict that ultimately matters.

## How it's used in practice

The dominant pattern is **formal verification as the rescue tool late in the cycle**. Potdar et al. at Intel 'present a case study demonstrating the successful utilization of last-minute formal verification techniques near tape-in date,' finding starvations and deadlocks through 'clever bug hunting strategies involving existing RTL assertions and high-level end-to-end checkers' [When Last Minute FV Strikes Gold, 2023]. Jain, Gupta and KiranKumar extend the same theme to the *post-silicon* phase with a 'stepwise guide to apply formal verification in post-silicon phase to reproduce post silicon failures in pre-silicon, validate the bug fixes, build a comprehensive solution to uncover additional vulnerabilities' — and their punchline is that 'design size need not be a limiting factor in FV application for post-silicon debug' [Never too late with formal, 2022]. Yeung, Eslinger and Hou generalize the practice into a 'spiral refinement methodology' that captures success factors and 'gradually improves each of the factors to zero-in on the critical bugs,' demonstrated on a real DDR3 controller silicon bug [Formal Verification Experiences: Spiral Refinement Methodology, 2021].

A second pattern is **bridging the verification-to-validation gap**. Dhok and Paulraj describe automotive RADAR bitfield verification at NXP: 'faster silicon bring-up requires pre-verified register bitfield settings,' but 'there is a gap in terms of correctness of block configuration settings given by design owners to SW API team,' because those settings were unverified for combined functional scenarios. Their fix is a Python script that converts the bitfield settings to SystemVerilog so the existing UVM environment verifies them, then converts them back — letting 'on the day one of Silicon bring-up, the pre-verified RADAR sub' block run with confidence [Automotive RADAR Bitfields Verification, 2023]. Ciaglia, Winkler and Kundrata attack the same seam from the firmware side at ams-OSRAM, arguing there is 'a considerable time gap between the availability of an IC design in simulation versus its availability in FPGA or silicon' and a 'tool boundary because SystemVerilog test benches... cannot be directly re-used in the tool flows used for FPGA or silicon-based firmware development' [Unified Firmware Debug throughout SoC Development Lifecycle, 2022].

The third pattern is **system-level rigor that simulation alone could not deliver**. Chatterjee, Parsons and Dhodhi at NVIDIA tackle system-level deadlocks, which 'can often escape pre-silicon verification and even emulation testing,' with a FIFO virtual-channel dependency graph that catches them before they cost a respin — because 'a possible re-spin could cost the chip manufacturer millions of dollars' [Mechanism to Generate FIFO VC Dependency Graph, 2021]. Sinha and Christie make the CDC/RDC version of the same argument: 'pre-silicon or sometimes post-silicon bug cost an entire respin due to meta-stability issues or due to glitches in the clock-reset paths,' driving them to validate CDC/RDC assumptions with an autonomous assertion-based flow [A step towards Zero Silicon Bugs, 2020].

## Pitfalls and where the field is heading

The biggest pitfall is **relying on dynamic simulation as the only safety net**. The Intel and Mentor authors are emphatic that simulation alone, however thorough, will let corner-case bugs 'fall through the cracks and make their way into the silicon due to inexhaustive coverage.' Rawat, Arya and Anuroop R document the low-power variant of this lesson at Intel graphics: static low-power checks 'are designed to verify low power architectures and capturing some of the critical issues early,' but only when the team ran them as a sign-off [Recipe for bug hunting, 2021]. Handover, Ayari and Yeung's 'deep sea fishing' bug-hunt radar formalizes the meta-lesson: post-silicon bugs 'happen deep in functional operation under unusual combinations of events and scenarios,' so sophisticated approaches — not brute simulation — are required [Formal Verification Experiences: Silicon Bug Hunt with "Deep Sea Fishing", 2020].

The field is heading toward **portable test intent across the whole life cycle** and **richer mixed-domain case studies**. Bhatnagar and Fricano show PSS as the unifying layer for performance, DV, and post-silicon on one fabric. Ehrlich, Nguyen and Vörtler demonstrate UVM-SystemC hardware-in-the-loop simulations for accelerated co-verification of automotive airbag systems at Infineon, where 'classical simulation-based development flow' could no longer handle the safety-critical mixed-signal real-time system [UVM-SystemC based hardware in the loop simulations, 2014]. Rathi and Chandran push system-level constrained random as a block-level technique that must scale to system level despite the combinatorial explosion [System-Level Random Verification, 2019]. Sachtjen and Gaubatz extend the lens to prototype debuggers for system validation, sharing 'strategies employed and lessons learned' across ASIC and FPGA silicon debug [Not Just for Hardware Debug, 2015]. The unifying takeaway is modest and hard-won: methodology is necessary, but the chips that ship are the ones whose teams treated verification as a life-cycle discipline, not a phase.

## Grounded in these DVCon papers

- **Product Life Cycle of an Interconnect Bus: A Portable Stimulus Methodology for Performance Modeling, Design Verification, and Post-Silicon Validation** (2019, DVCon US) — Gaurav Bhatnagar and Courtney Fricano. PSS-based traffic generator carrying one Analog Devices interconnect fabric from SystemC performance analysis through DV to post-silicon validation.
- **SoC Verification Enablement Using HM Model** (2019, DVCon India) — Vineet Tanwar, Chirag Kedia and Rahul Gupta. UVM-based hard-macro model substituted for unavailable HM RTL at Qualcomm, enabling SoC verification and first-pass success.
- **When Last Minute FV Strikes Gold: A Case Study on Finding Starvations & Deadlocks in a Project Nearing Tape-in** (2023, DVCon India) — Abhishek Potdar, Sachin Kumawat and Sudhanshu Srivastava. Last-minute formal bug hunt near tape-in at Intel using RTL assertions and end-to-end checkers.
- **Automotive RADAR Bitfields Verification to support Validation of Silicon bring-up** (2023, DVCon India) — Amol Dhok and Paulraj M K. Python-bridged bitfield verification at NXP that closed the design-to-SW-API gap before RADAR silicon bring-up.
- **Unified Firmware Debug throughout SoC Development Lifecycle** (2022, DVCon Europe) — Dimitri Ciaglia, Thomas Winkler and Jurica Kundrata. ams-OSRAM case study attacking the simulation-to-FPGA/silicon tool boundary in firmware debug.
- **Never too late with formal: Stepwise guide for applying formal verification in post-silicon phase to avoid re-spins** (2022, DVCon US) — Anshul Jain, Aarti Gupta and Achutha KiranKumar V M. Stepwise guide to post-silicon formal verification at Intel for root-causing failures and validating fixes.
- **Formal Verification Experiences: Spiral Refinement Methodology for Silicon Bug Hunt** (2021, DVCon US) — Ping Yeung, Mark Eslinger and Jin Hou. Spiral-refinement bug-hunt methodology, demonstrated on a DDR3 controller silicon bug.
- **Recipe for bug hunting: Tips & Tricks for Low power silicon sign-off** (2021, DVCon US) — Monika Rawat, Dipankar Narendra Arya and Anuroop R. Static low-power checks and case studies from a complex Intel graphics SoC.
- **Mechanism to Generate FIFO VC Dependency Graph and Its Application to System Level Deadlock Verification** (2021, DVCon US) — Debarshi Chatterjee, Chad Parsons and Siddhanth Dhodhi. FIFO virtual-channel dependency graph at NVIDIA catching system-level deadlocks that escaped pre-silicon and emulation.
- **Formal Verification Experiences: Silicon Bug Hunt with "Deep Sea Fishing"** (2020, DVCon Europe) — Mark Handover and Abdelouahab Ayari and Ping Yeung. 'Deep sea fishing' radar for post-silicon formal bug hunts on complex SoC designs.
- **A step towards Zero Silicon Bugs using Assertion based Assumption Validation** (2020, DVCon Europe) — Rohit Kumar Sinha and Babu Christie. Autonomous CDC/RDC assumption-validation flow at Intel India to prevent meta-stability respins.
- **System-Level Random Verification: How it should be done** (2019, DVCon US) — Madhusudan Rathi and Ashok Chandran. Scaling constrained-random verification from block to system level on configurable SoCs.
- **Advances in RF Transceiver SoC Verification: A Walk-Through over a 2.4 GHz Multi-Modal Integrated Transceiver Verification Cycle** (2017, DVCon US) — Charul Agrawal, Ashwin Vijayan and and Jakub Dudek. ADI case study on a 2.4 GHz multi-modal RF transceiver bridging design, verification, and firmware teams.
- **UVM-SystemC based hardware in the loop simulations for accelerated Co-Verification** (2014, DVCon Europe) — Paul Ehrlich, Thang Nguyen and and Thilo Vörtler. Infineon/Fraunhofer HIL co-verification of a safety-critical airbag mixed-signal SoC.
- **Not Just for Hardware Debug: Prototype Debuggers for System Validation and Optimization** (2015, DVCon US) — Michael Sachtjen and Joe Gaubatz. Lessons from silicon debuggers applied to system-level validation and optimization across ASIC and FPGA.
