# AMS (Analog/Mixed-Signal) Verification

> *Analog designers trust SPICE. Digital verification engineers trust UVM. When your chip has both — and every modern SoC does — you have to convince two cultures that don't speak the same language to share a testbench. SPICE simulates a few microseconds per hour; event-driven SystemVerilog eats millions of cycles a second. Plug them together and the analog solver becomes the entire bottleneck, while the digital team waits and the project schedule slips. Real Number Modeling was supposed to fix this — abstract the analog away into fast event-driven signals — but it leaks precision exactly where the analog designer cared most. The state of the art is a perpetual compromise between speed and accuracy, between reusing a digital UVM environment and respecting the electrical reality of a regulator, a PLL, or an antenna. So how do you build a verification flow that mixes RTL with analog without losing your mind — or your silicon? Let's dig in.*

## What it is

**AMS (analog/mixed-signal) verification** is the discipline of verifying chips that combine digital RTL with analog blocks — ADCs, DACs, PLLs, voltage regulators, RF front-ends, and the power-management ICs (PMICs) that have become mandatory in almost every analog-centric design [A Configurable, Re-usable UVM Environment Coupled with Advanced Spice Simulator for Analog and Mixed-Signal Verification of a Display PMIC, 2024]. The defining difficulty is that no standard methodology exists for it — digital verification has UVM, but analog verification is still largely ad hoc, with each chip manufacturer using a different mix of abstraction levels [A Framework for AMS Verification IP development with SystemVerilog, UVM and Verilog-AMS, 2014].

The central abstraction is the trade between **SPICE-level simulation** (accurate but slow) and **event-driven** simulation (fast but discrete). SPICE solvers for the analog portion run on entirely different timescales than event-based Verilog for the digital portion, so the two schedules diverge and turnaround times balloon [DV UVM based AMS co-simulation and verification methodology for mixed signal designs, 2024]. The bridge technologies are **Verilog-AMS** and, increasingly, **SystemVerilog Real Number Modeling (SV-RNM)**, which represents analog signals as real-valued numbers driven on every clock edge — up to 100,000× faster than SPICE [With great power comes great responsibility: A method to verify PMICs using UVM-MS, 2014] [Real Number Modeling HOW TO VERIFY MIXED-SIGNAL BEHAVIOR USING EVENT-BASED SIMULATION, 2013].

The justification for going fast is that mixed-signal SoCs are no longer decomposable. Digital and analog functions used to be separable, with each side making assumptions about the other; today's designs have analog functions implemented digitally and digital logic controlling analog peripherals so tightly that the assumptions break [Mixed Signal Verification of Dynamic Adaptive Power Management in Low Power SoC, 2010]. Modern ASSPs routinely mix a Cortex-M3 MCU with ADCs, DACs, and PLLs on one die, demanding that the metric-driven verification (MDV) practices from the digital world be extended to analog content [Metric Driven Mixed-Signal Verification Methodology and Practices for Complex Mixed-signal ASSPs, 2014].

## How it's used in practice

The dominant pattern is to **reuse a UVM environment and extend it with AMS awareness**, rather than write a parallel analog testbench. An AMS Verification IP (VIP) wraps an analog model with the familiar driver/monitor/coverage structure, using Verilog-AMS to interact with the analog interface while UVM handles the digital side [A Framework for AMS Verification IP development with SystemVerilog, UVM and Verilog-AMS, 2014]. The same UVM environment can then be re-pointed at SPICE: the PMIC team reuses its DMS (digital mixed-signal) environment, swaps SV-RNM models for SPICE in a subset of the scenarios, and runs true AMS verification on power-up/power-down, I2C transactions, dynamic voltage scaling, and protection logic [A Configurable, Re-usable UVM Environment Coupled with Advanced Spice Simulator for Analog and Mixed-Signal Verification of a Display PMIC, 2024]. Co-simulation methodologies extend this further, coupling UVM with an advanced SPICE solver to close the schedule gap between analog and digital teams [DV UVM based AMS co-simulation and verification methodology for mixed signal designs, 2024].

**Real Number Modeling** is the workhorse abstraction. IBM's deployment moved from SPICE-level and conservative electrical co-simulation to Verilog-AMS real number models running entirely in event-based simulation, verifying a full mixed-signal IC [Real Number Modeling HOW TO VERIFY MIXED-SIGNAL BEHAVIOR USING EVENT-BASED SIMULATION, 2013]. RNM works even for RF circuits, where PLLs and mixers were historically considered too precise for real-number treatment — Analog Devices demonstrated RNM models of RF blocks providing a fast, efficient simulation platform for test and firmware development [Real Number Modeling of RF Circuits, 2017]. For regulators and PMICs, the approach is to identify the dominant electrical property of each interface — usually voltage or current — and build a behavioral representation in Verilog-AMS/wreal [With great power comes great responsibility: A method to verify PMICs using UVM-MS, 2014].

Coverage and checking have to span the analog/digital boundary. **Mixed-signal assertions** extend SVA to reference analog node voltages and currents inside SPICE subcircuits, with helper functions like `cds_analog_get_value` and `cds_analog_exists` letting one assertion work whether its target is digital or analog [Mixed Signal Assertion-Based Verification, 2011]. State-space techniques using the SystemVerilog-DC extensions model analog behavior mathematically rather than electrically, allowing formal-style analysis of regulators [Mixed Signal Verification of a Voltage Regulator using a State Space Approach and the SV--DC extensions, 2016]. And the **DVS interface element** handles multi-power-domain mixed-signal designs where supply sensitivity, level shifting, and isolation all interact at the analog boundary [DVS Interface Element, 2016].

## Pitfalls and where the field is heading

The recurring pitfall is **accuracy leakage**. RNM is enormously faster than SPICE but loses the electrical precision that catches subtle analog bugs — so teams have to choose, scenario by scenario, what to verify at RNM speed and what to verify at SPICE accuracy, often reusing the same UVM environment for both [DV UVM based AMS co-simulation and verification methodology for mixed signal designs, 2024]. The lack of a standard methodology amplifies this: there is no analog equivalent of UVM, and "there is no standard methodology or re-usable component for AMS verification" is a complaint that has echoed across more than a decade of papers [A Framework for AMS Verification IP development with SystemVerilog, UVM and Verilog-AMS, 2014].

The tooling gaps are real but closing. **SystemVerilog user-defined nettypes (SV-UDN)** and packages like `SV_LUT` are extending RNM to handle complex analog behaviors via lookup tables, improving integration with UVM [SV_LUT: A SystemVerilog Look Up Table package for developing complex AMS Real Number Modeling, 2024]. Testbench automation is the remaining frontier — while AMS modeling has converged on Verilog-AMS and SV-RNM and behavioral model tooling exists, UVM testbench generation for AMS is still largely manual, unlike the digital world where TLM models can drive both RTL and UVM testbench generation [UVM Testbench Automation for AMS Designs, 2024]. Connecting UVM to mixed-signal designs that use Verilog-AMS remains an open problem with multiple competing approaches and no standard method [Connecting UVM with Mixed-Signal Design, 2017].

The trajectory is toward **deeper metric-driven verification** for analog content and broader reuse of UVM infrastructure. The shift is visible in how PMIC verification now routinely starts as DMS using SV-RNM, then is selectively escalated to true AMS for the scenarios that need SPICE accuracy — the same env, different solver [A Configurable, Re-usable UVM Environment Coupled with Advanced Spice Simulator for Analog and Mixed-Signal Verification of a Display PMIC, 2024]. The longer-term promise is that AMS inherits the full metric-driven verification discipline — planning, coverage, regressions — that has long been standard for digital SoCs [Metric Driven Mixed-Signal Verification Methodology and Practices for Complex Mixed-signal ASSPs, 2014].

- **Mixed Signal Verification of a Voltage Regulator using a State Space Approach and the SV--DC extensions** (2016, DVCon US) — Rajat Mitra. State-space mathematical modeling of analog behavior using SystemVerilog-DC extensions.
- **With great power comes great responsibility: A method to verify PMICs using UVM-MS** (2014, DVCon US) — Dor Spigel and Moshik Hershcovitch. Behavioral RNM of PMIC interfaces up to 100,000× faster than SPICE.
- **SV_LUT: A SystemVerilog Look Up Table package for developing complex AMS Real Number Modeling** (2024, DVCon US) — FNU Farshad, Shafaitul Islam Surush and Simul Barua. Lookup-table package extending SV-RNM and SV-UDN for complex analog behavior.
- **UVM Testbench Automation for AMS Designs** (2024, DVCon US) — J. B. David and H. Chang. Identifies testbench automation as the remaining AMS productivity gap.
- **Real Number Modeling of RF Circuits** (2017, DVCon US) — Jakub Dudek, Joshua Nekl and Keith O'Donoghue. Demonstrates RNM as a fast platform for RF PLLs and mixers.
- **Metric Driven Mixed-Signal Verification Methodology and Practices for Complex Mixed-signal ASSPs** (2014, DVCon US) — Frank Yang, Andy Sha and Morton Zhao. Extends digital MDV practices to a Cortex-M3 plus analog peripherals ASSP.
- **Mixed Signal Verification of Dynamic Adaptive Power Management in Low Power SoC** (2010, DVCon US) — Neyaz Khan. Early articulation of why modern mixed-signal designs can no longer be decomposed into separable analog/digital functions.
- **Connecting UVM with Mixed-Signal Design** (2017, DVCon US) — Ivica Ignjić. Catalogs the competing approaches for connecting UVM with Verilog-AMS designs.
- **DVS Interface Element - A Novel Approach in Multi-Power Domain, Mixed-Signal Design Verification** (2016, DVCon US) — Vijay Kumar Sankaran, Ji Du and Qingyu Lin. Handles supply sensitivity, level shifting, and isolation at the mixed-signal boundary.
