# Verilog-AMS for Mixed-Signal Verification

> *"Verilog-AMS is the language that refuses to die. Back in 2013, IBM's Tom Cole stood up at DVCon and described the painful pivot away from SPICE-level co-simulation toward real-number models in Verilog-AMS, running both analog and digital sides in one event-driven engine — and a dozen years later, the same language is still the lingua franca of mixed-signal testbenches. Why? Because Verilog-AMS is the one place where the digital UVM world and the analog electrical world actually touch. Its `wreal` nets and `disciplines` give you a disciplined way to thread real numbers through the same simulation that's wiggling your RTL pins, and its connect modules silently translate discrete to continuous and back. Real Number Modeling (RNM) in pure SystemVerilog has been eating Verilog-AMS's lunch for speed, but when you need an analog solver in the loop — for a regulator's feedback loop, a PMIC's protection logic, or a serdes eye — Verilog-AMS is still where the buck stops. So what's actually under the hood, and where does it still win? Let's dig in."*

## What it is

**Verilog-AMS** is the mixed-signal extension to the Verilog family: the complete Verilog (IEEE 1364-2005) specification, the analog modeling language Verilog-A, plus extensions for mixed-signal description. As Cole, Queen and Kautzman laid out, "Verilog-AMS includes *wreal* net types, useful for structurally connecting design elements together using real numbers," and it adds the unique notion of **disciplines** — a declaration that defines the domain (analog or digital) and the nature (voltage and/or current) of a net, plus attributes like tolerances [Real Number Modeling HOW TO VERIFY MIXED-SIGNAL BEHAVIOR USING EVENT-BASED SIMULATION, 2013]. Verilog-A, the analog-only ancestor folded into the AMS standard, is for true analog solver work and is *not* used to build real-number models — that distinction matters when you pick your abstraction.

The glue that makes AMS useful in a UVM testbench is the **connect module**. A connect module defines the conversion between disciplines — real to electrical, or logic to electrical — and is inserted automatically at the boundary where a UVM environment (discrete domain) meets a module with continuous-discipline ports [A Framework for AMS Verification IP development with SystemVerilog, UVM and Verilog-AMS, 2014]. No connect modules are instantiated when the UVM side talks only to discrete-domain models; the moment you point a driver at a real electrical port, the AMS machinery kicks in. That seam is exactly where the speed/accuracy tradeoff lives: SPICE-accurate electrical simulation runs hours per microsecond, while wreal and discrete real numbers run at digital-event speed.

## How it's used in practice

The dominant pattern is to wrap Verilog-AMS models inside a **UVM-MS VIP**. Bartley and Gandhi's framework treats the UVM transaction as a discrete-time real value, then lets Verilog-AMS do the D/A conversion through real-value-modeling (RVM) techniques — the analog driver and monitor become UVM components, and assertions can fire on analog values sampled through an ADC in the testbench [A Framework for AMS Verification IP development with SystemVerilog, UVM and Verilog-AMS, 2014]. Sonara et al. scale this to a full-chip coverage-driven environment that mixes SystemVerilog user-defined nettypes, Verilog-AMS, and transistor models in any combination, finding bugs that pure digital verification missed on a Broadcom mixed-signal SoC [SCALABLE, RE-USABLE UVM DMS AMS BASED VERIFICATION METHODOLOGY FOR MIXED-SIGNAL SOCS, 2019].

For power-management ICs, the methodology crystallized early. Spigel and Hershcovitch verified a Microsemi PMU by identifying the dominant electrical property of each interface — current or voltage — and building a behavioral wreal representation that ran up to 100,000× faster than SPICE [With great power comes great responsibility: A method to verify PMICs using UVM-MS, 2014]. Sankaran and colleagues extended Verilog-AMS into the low-power world with the **DVS interface element**, which handles supply sensitivity, level shifting, and isolation in multi-power-domain mixed-signal designs where CPF/UPF intent and AMS co-simulation collide [DVS Interface Element, 2016] [A Holistic Approach to Low-Power, Mixed-Signal Design Verification, 2016]. And for the trickiest interfaces — like MIPI D-PHY, where LP sequencing hands off to HS differential signalling — Mekkadan shows Verilog-AMS co-simulation catching modes that pure behavioral RTL simply cannot [Functional Verification of CSI-2 Rx-PHY using AMS Co-simulations, 2014].

## Pitfalls and where the field is heading

The eternal pitfall is **accuracy loss**. Pure SV-RNM is faster and more digital-friendly, but it leaks electrical precision exactly where the analog designer cares most; Verilog-AMS keeps the analog solver in the loop and stays honest, at a speed cost. There is also no analog UVM — "no standard methodology or re-usable component for AMS verification" was the 2014 complaint and still echoes [A Framework for AMS Verification IP development with SystemVerilog, UVM and Verilog-AMS, 2014]. Tooling maturity varies: David and Chang note that while AMS modeling has converged on Verilog-AMS and SV-RNM, and behavioral-model productivity tools exist, **testbench automation** for AMS is the remaining gap — digital teams get TLM-driven UVM generation for free, AMS teams still hand-write it [UVM Testbench Automation for AMS Designs, 2024].

The trajectory is twofold. First, **UVM frameworks that natively host analog models**: Barua, Kabir and Chang's AMSV utility leverages SystemVerilog OOP to monitor and control analog behavioral models inside UVM, aiming at AI, IoT, and automotive mixed-signal verification [Addressing Advanced Mixed-Signal Verification Scenarios by Developing a UVM Framework for Analog Models, 2025]. Second, **reuse of digital checking techniques on analog content**: Sharma et al.'s adaptive glitch detectors use compact Verilog-AMS assertion and checker modules to automate module verification at the SoC level, cutting manual SoC-level checks [Accelerating Silicon Bug Detection and Optimizing Execution Flow through Intelligent Adaptive Glitch Detectors in AMS Verification, 2024], and Brownnell and Schmitt describe "digitizing" mixed-signal verification by importing UVM-MS, randomization, and metric-driven discipline into analog teams [Digitizing Mixed Signal Verification, 2014]. O'Riordan and Bhattacharya even extend PSL/SVA assertion semantics into SPICE itself — pragmas inside subcircuits — pushing the AMS assertion idea all the way down to the electrical solver [PSL/SVA Assertions in SPICE, 2012]. Verilog-AMS isn't going anywhere; it's being absorbed into a broader, metric-driven AMS flow.
