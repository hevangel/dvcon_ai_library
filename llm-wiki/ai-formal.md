# AI for Formal Verification

> Formal verification has always had an awkward image problem: it is mathematically the most powerful tool in the building, and yet most engineers treat it like a black box that only the central FV priesthood can operate. The bottleneck is rarely the solver — it is the human. Writing properties, extracting a verification plan, setting up the environment, debugging the inevitable CEX waveforms, and arguing with the tool about which `assume` is too strong all consume hours that should not exist. So it was probably inevitable that generative AI would come for formal next. The pitch is seductive: a Copilot that drafts your SVA, a GenAI assistant that reads your spec and emits a verification plan, an LLM that stares at a counter-example and tells you the root cause. The reality is more nuanced, but the early results are no longer toy demos. Let's dig in.

## What it is and where the human bottleneck lives

**AI for formal verification** covers a family of techniques in which machine-learning or large-language-model components sit alongside the classical formal flow — property specification, environment setup, constraint authoring, coverage analysis, and counterexample debugging. The motivation is straightforward: formal property verification (FPV) is conceptually distant enough from simulation that deployment has historically been confined to specialist teams, even though modern EDA tools have made large usability leaps [Making Formal Property Verification Mainstream: An Intel Graphics Experience, 2017]. The expense is not the proof; it is the time a skilled engineer spends extracting intent from a spec, encoding it as `cover` and `assume` statements, and triaging false failures.

Generative AI changes that equation by automating the natural-language-to-property translation. **SIGMA** is representative of the new generation: a GenAI-augmented formal sign-off flow that integrates generative models, automation scripts, and Copilot components to extract the verification plan, set up the environment, and develop formal claims — collectively reducing manual effort and improving coverage [SIGMA, 2026]. The same pattern appears in coverage closure, where **Unified Coverage Methodology** shows that AI-ML technology across simulation, formal, and emulation platforms can shrink the 20–25% of the verification cycle traditionally consumed by coverage closure [Unified Coverage Methodology, 2024].

A complementary thread uses formal *as the judge* of AI-generated artifacts. **All Artificial, Less Intelligence** runs formal verification against a 60,000-design dataset of SystemVerilog emitted by four different LLMs, classifying each design as vulnerable or CWE-free against ten Common Weakness Enumerations [All Artificial, Less Intelligence, 2024]. The pairing is significant: formal gives the LLM a ground truth that simulation cannot, and the LLM gives formal a corpus large enough to expose systematic blind spots.

## How it shows up in real designs

The DVCon corpus documents AI-for-formal in two flavors. The first is *AI assistance layered on top of a conventional FPV flow*, with the human still in the loop. SIGMA's Copilot-style components and the AI-ML coverage tooling in Unified Coverage Methodology fit here — they accelerate the engineer's existing workflow rather than replace it [SIGMA, 2026][Unified Coverage Methodology, 2024]. **Comprehensive & Configurable Ethernet IP Verification Strategy** is a field example, combining UVM, Python modeling, and AI-enhanced formal debug to close sign-off on a heavily parameterized Ethernet IP [Comprehensive & Configurable Ethernet IP Verification Strategy, 2025].

The second flavor is harder: using formal methods to verify designs whose entire reason for existing is AI/ML compute. **Dot Product Accumulate Systolic (DPAS)** units — the matrix multiply-add engines inside modern GPUs and AI accelerators — are the canonical case. **Raising the Bar** lays out a sign-off methodology for DPAS, emphasizing that the traditional "verify RTL against a golden spec" approach breaks down when the spec model itself is untrustworthy, and proposes integrating ML techniques into the verification flow [Raising the Bar, 2023]. **Complexity Conquered** extends the work to systolic controllers, addressing the convergence hurdles FPV faces on the intricate control logic of DPAS designs [Complexity Conquered, 2024]. **Achieving End-to-End Formal Verification of Large Floating-Point DPAS Units** pushes the methodology to full end-to-end formal equivalence checking, with trusted reference models and a soundness check on the formal setup itself [Achieving End-to-End Formal Verification of Large Floating-Point Dot Product Accumulate Systolic Units, 2024]. **Navigating Complexity to Convergence** tackles the single-precision fused multiply-add (FMA) units that anchor AI/ML accelerators, where deep pipelines and extended exponent widths explode the symbolic state space [Navigating Complexity to Convergence: Formal Verification for Single Precision FMAs, 2025].

The intersection with the broader design is unavoidable. **Analogous Alignments** brings formal to test chips, where loosely verified pre-silicon work turns into expensive re-spins, and **A Systematic Take on Addressing Dynamic CDC Verification Challenges** shows formal and simulation working together to close CDC protocol verification [Analogous Alignments, 2024][A Systematic Take on Addressing Dynamic CDC Verification Challenges, 2019].

## Pitfalls, scalability, and where the field is heading

The biggest trap is conflating "AI helped me set up formal" with "formal proved the design." An LLM that drafts a plausible `assume` can silently over-constrain the proof and manufacture a false pass — and GenAI through the lens of formal is precisely the experiment that surfaces how often that happens [All Artificial, Less Intelligence, 2024]. The mitigation is the same one formal has always demanded: independent review of constraints, soundness checks on the proof environment, and trusted reference models against which equivalence is checked [Achieving End-to-End Formal Verification of Large Floating-Point Dot Product Accumulate Systolic Units, 2024]. Generative assistants compress setup time but do not remove the obligation to audit what they produced.

Scalability is the other open wound. DPAS, FMA, and floating-point datapaths are exactly the designs where the symbolic state space explodes, and even the most polished methodology has to lean on assume-guarantee reasoning, helper assertions, and case-splitting to converge [Navigating Complexity to Convergence: Formal Verification for Single Precision FMAs, 2025][Complexity Conquered, 2024]. ML can help rank which abstractions to try first, but it cannot repeal the underlying complexity. The field is heading toward hybrid flows in which a GenAI layer handles planning and authoring, classical formal handles the proof, and ML-driven coverage tooling decides when enough has been proven — a stack where each layer compensates for the weaknesses of the others [Unified Coverage Methodology, 2024][SIGMA, 2026].

- **Raising the Bar: Achieving Formal Verification Sign-Off for Complex Algorithmic Designs, with a Dot Product Accumulate Case Study** (2023, DVCon India) — Disha Puri, Madhurima Eranki and Shravya Jampana. Lays out a formal sign-off methodology for DPAS units and motivates ML integration.
- **Complexity Conquered: Pioneering Formal Verification Methods for Systolic Controllers in Advanced Computing** (2024, DVCon India) — Sarsij Saurabh, Rahul Dabur and Tushar Agarwal. Extends FPV methodology to the control logic of DPAS engines.
- **Unified Coverage Methodology: Accelerated Coverage Closure at SoC and IP level** (2024, DVCon India) — Prateek Jain and Ajay Goyal. Uses AI-ML across simulation, formal, and emulation to compress coverage closure.
- **A Systematic Take on Addressing Dynamic CDC Verification Challenges** (2019, DVCon US) — Sukriti Bisht, Sulabh Kumar Khare and Ashish Hari. Combines formal and simulation for CDC protocol verification.
- **Achieving End-to-End Formal Verification of Large Floating-Point Dot Product Accumulate Systolic Units** (2024, DVCon US) — Emiliano Morini, Bill Zorn and Disha Puri. End-to-end formal equivalence checking flow for large floating-point DPAS units with soundness checks.
- **Navigating Complexity to Convergence: Formal Verification for Single Precision FMAs** (2025, DVCon India) — Swaresh Phadke and Madhurima E. Formal convergence methodology for single-precision fused multiply-add units in AI/ML accelerators.
- **Analogous Alignments: Digital "Formally" meets Analog** (2024, DVCon Europe) — Hansa Mohanty and Deepak Narayan Gadde. Left-shifts formal onto test chips to prevent expensive re-spins.
- **SIGMA: Sign-off Intelligence with GenAI for Methodical Assurance in Formal Verification** (2026, DVCon US) — R C Sanjay Krushnan, Moola Jeevan Chaitanya Goud and Sakthivel Ramaiah. A GenAI-augmented formal sign-off flow that automates planning, setup, and claim development.
- **Making Formal Property Verification Mainstream: An Intel® Graphics Experience** (2017, DVCon US) — M Achutha KiranKumar V, Erik Seligman and Aarti Gupta. The historical case for deploying FPV beyond specialist teams.
- **Coverage Data Exchange is no robbery…or is it?** (2015, DVCon US) — Darron May and Samiran Laha. UCIS-based coverage exchange that underpins unified AI-ML coverage flows.
- **Leveraging IP-XACT standardized IP interfaces for rapid IP integration** (2014, DVCon US) — David Murray and Simon Rance. Standardized IP metadata that AI tooling increasingly consumes.
- **Comprehensive & Configurable Ethernet IP Verification Strategy** (2025, DVCon Europe) — Tom O'Connor, Sameh El-Ashry and Atif Ansari. UVM, Python modeling, and AI-enhanced formal for heavily parameterized Ethernet IP.
- **Functional Twin: A Framework for Reusable Virtual Electronic Control Units** (2025, DVCon Europe) — Sacha Loitz, Torsten Hermann and Martin Hruschka. Digital-twin framework for software-defined vehicle validation adjacent to formal sign-off.
