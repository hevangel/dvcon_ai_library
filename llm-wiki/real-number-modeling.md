# Real Number Modeling (RNM) for AMS

> *"SPICE is the truth, and SPICE is unusable. That's the central tension of mixed-signal verification in one sentence. A transistor-level simulation of a PLL or a PMIC can take days for a few microseconds of real time, and the digital testbench — running millions of cycles a second — sits idle waiting for it. Real Number Modeling is the engineer's compromise: replace the analog solver with event-driven simulation that carries analog values as floating-point numbers, sampled on clock edges. You lose the continuous-time matrix solving, you lose a little precision, and in return you gain up to 100,000× in speed. IBM was deploying RNM in production in 2013; Analog Devices was modeling RF PLLs and mixers with it in 2017; Samsung was using SV-RNM on PMICs for SSDs in 2022. The reason this page exists separately from AMS verification is that RNM is a specific abstraction with specific failure modes — and if you don't know where it leaks, it will hand you a passing testbench over a broken chip. Let's dig in."*

## What it is

**Real Number Modeling (RNM)** is a mixed-signal abstraction that borrows from both worlds: the values are continuous — floating-point real numbers, like analog — but time is discrete and events drive the solver, like digital [Real Number Modeling HOW TO VERIFY MIXED-SIGNAL BEHAVIOR USING EVENT-BASED SIMULATION, 2013]. An analog solver must solve the entire analog system matrix at every step because every element can influence every other element; an RNM model breaks that coupling by declaring a signal flow direction, so the value just propagates forward on each triggering event. The classic Verilog-AMS implementation uses the **`wreal`** net type to structurally wire real-number ports together, with disciplines defining the nature (voltage, current) of each net [Real Number Modeling HOW TO VERIFY MIXED-SIGNAL BEHAVIOR USING EVENT-BASED SIMULATION, 2013]. Modern SystemVerilog added user-defined nettypes (SV-UDN) and user-defined resolution functions (UDR), which is why **SV-RNM** has overtaken Verilog-AMS/wreal as the preferred form — it integrates cleanly with UVM and supports struct-based nettypes like the `{Voltage, Current, Resistance}` (VIR) format [SV VQC UDN for Modeling Switch-Capacitorbased Circuits…, 2023].

The reason teams reach for RNM is the **speed gap**. A power-management unit can contain thousands of control bits and complex digital-to-analog handshake mechanisms, and SPICE-level co-simulation "took forever and critical bugs were always found near the tapeout," with the vast majority being integration errors — control bits hooked up wrong, drivers mismatched to loads [With great power comes great responsibility: A method to verify PMICs using UVM-MS, 2014]. RNM models are reported to run up to 100,000× faster than SPICE, which is the only reason you can put a PMIC into a regression at all [With great power comes great responsibility: A method to verify PMICs using UVM-MS, 2014]. The same logic applies to RF: Dudek et al. show that even PLLs and RF mixers — long thought too precise for real-number treatment — are excellent RNM candidates, because the alternative (AMS or Fast-SPICE) is too slow to support firmware development at RF speeds [Real Number Modeling of RF Circuits, 2017].

## How it's used in practice

The dominant pattern is to **swap an RNM model in for SPICE inside a UVM environment**, run the full digital-style regression at RNM speed, then swap SPICE back in for a handful of targeted tests where accuracy matters. The PMIC team at Meta describes exactly this: AMS verification with the same UVM-based testbench, dynamically switching between RNM and SPICE netlists per scenario so the regression pays for SPICE only on the cases that need it [DV UVM based AMS co-simulation and verification methodology for mixed signal designs, 2024]. The same DMS-then-AMS pattern shows up for a Display PMIC, where basic power-up/power-down, I2C transactions, dynamic voltage scaling, and fault protection run as Digital Mixed-Signal with SV-RNM, with a subset escalated to true AMS via an advanced SPICE simulator [A Configurable, Re-usable UVM Environment Coupled with Advanced Spice Simulator for Analog and Mixed-Signal Verification of a Display PMIC, 2024]. For a PMIC designed for SSD applications, Kumar et al. frame the choice as three challenges solved by one methodology: runtime, register-bank default-value checking, and coverage — all addressed by SV-RNM modeling [Harnessing SV-RNM Based Modelling and Simulation Methodology for Verifying a Complex PMIC designed for SSD Applications, 2022].

Building accurate RNM models is its own craft. For nonlinear analog behavior, **lookup tables** are the workhorse: Farshad et al. introduce `SV_LUT`, a SystemVerilog LUT package that exports SPICE data to CSV, ingests it as a parameterized LUT, and interpolates on the fly — demonstrated on a PTAT core where temperature drives the lookup [SV_LUT: A SystemVerilog Look Up Table package for developing complex AMS Real Number Modeling, 2024]. For load effects, Wang's VIR-format SV-UDN `{Voltage, Current, Resistance}` has a "natural advantage" over scalar wreal because the structure explicitly carries resistance — and with internal clocks it extends cleanly to capacitors, inductors, and charge pumps [SV VQC UDN for Modeling Switch-Capacitorbased Circuits…, 2023]. Crucially, the same UVM randomization and coverage discipline that drives digital verification now applies to analog: Dudek et al. report functional coverage spanning channel number, packet length, frequency deviation, gain error, and input power, with cross coverage like input power vs. frequency deviation yielding real insight into the RF signal chain [Real Number Modeling of RF Circuits, 2017].

## Pitfalls and where the field is heading

The recurring pitfall is **accuracy leakage exactly where the analog designer cared most**. RNM is fast because it abstracts away the matrix solving, but that's also where the subtle bugs live — driver-loading effects, bidirectional signal flow, charge conservation. Sharma at Meta is blunt that the "digital and analog verification in parallel" approach exposes real verification gaps, which is why the new MSV testbench supports dynamic RNM/SPICE switching rather than committing to one abstraction [DV UVM based AMS co-simulation and verification methodology for mixed signal designs, 2024]. A second pitfall is **emulation incompatibility**: RNM uses the non-synthesizable `real` datatype, so an RNM model that works in simulation can't go on an emulator — Das describes migrating analog modeling to fixed-point arithmetic with Python-driven bit-width selection so the same blocks can run on the emulation platform for firmware-hardware co-verification [Analog Modelling to Suit Emulation for Hardware-Software Co-Verification, 2020]. A third is **model choice**: the Renesas team ran an explicit experiment with four PLL model types to figure out which modeling strategy fits which test, warning that the wrong choice "can potentially add up verification effort such as modeling maintenance" and slip the schedule [Automated Modeling Testbench Methodology Tested with four Types of PLL Models, 2023].

The field is heading toward **better integration with UVM, more systematic modeling libraries, and closing the testbench-automation gap**. David and Chang argue that AMS modeling itself has converged on Verilog-AMS and SV-RNM with solid simulator support, and that the remaining productivity gap is testbench automation — the digital world has SystemC TLM models that drive both RTL and UVM testbench generation, but the AMS world still builds testbenches largely by hand [UVM Testbench Automation for AMS Designs, 2024]. The early adopters are already reusing models across languages: McGrath et al. describe comprehensive AMS verification using Octave, RNM, and UVM together, with models reused across SystemVerilog and Matlab and randomized analog stimulus feeding analog functional coverage inside a UVM environment [Comprehensive AMS Verification using Octave, Real Number Modelling and UVM, 2015]. The long arc is that RNM is becoming the default fast abstraction for AMS — and as SV-UDN, library packages like `SV_LUT`, and UVM-MS patterns mature, the gap between "fast enough to regress" and "accurate enough to trust" keeps narrowing.
