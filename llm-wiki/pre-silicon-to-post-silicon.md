# Pre-Silicon to Post-Silicon Handoff

> *"You spent two years closing coverage at 100%, your regressions are green, the chip tapes out — and then the lab calls. Some subsystem fails at cold corner, or the OS hangs on the third boot, or a metastable bit flips in a power state you never simulated. The brutal truth of modern SoCs is that the pre-silicon world and the post-silicon world are different planets: simulators have full visibility but run at 10 Hz; silicon is fast but you can only watch what scan and on-chip debug let you see. As Chan, Mbaye, and Ang put it, 'when a test failure occurs, sometimes it is very hard to determine whether the issue is originated in the software code or the hardware code' [Is it a software bug? Is it a hardware bug?, 2022].

> The handoff between the two is where respins hide. Sinha and Christie describe the failure mode bluntly: 'we often get cases wherein pre-silicon or sometimes post-silicon bug cost an entire respin due to meta-stability issues or due to glitches in the clock-reset paths' [A step towards Zero Silicon Bugs using Assertion based Assumption Validation, 2020]. The pre-silicon team proved a property assuming some constraint; the post-silicon team violated that constraint and the chip died. The bug is in the seam.

> So what bridges that seam? Coverage that travels, tests that travel, and assumptions that get checked rather than waived. DVCon papers from 2014 through 2026 lay out a clear arc: PSS as a portable test medium, formal as a post-silicon debug weapon, AMS V&V reuse that gets you to 'one day bring up of silicon' [Analog Mixed Signal Verification and Validation(V&V) Methodology, 2024]. Let's see how the handoff is actually built."*

## What it is

The pre-silicon-to-post-silicon handoff is the set of **artifacts, methodologies, and shared infrastructure** that lets the verification work done in simulation, emulation, and FPGA prototyping pay off again in the lab. It has three load-bearing parts. First, **test content reuse**: the same tests, ideally the same binaries, run on the emulator and on the silicon so that a lab failure can be bisected against a known-good pre-silicon baseline. Chan et al. aim to 'run the same software testcase binary on both the silicon and emulation platform without recompile' — if the bug reproduces in emulation, it is a digital problem [Is it a software bug? Is it a hardware bug?, 2022]. Second, **coverage continuity**: the features you measured in simulation should map to features you can measure on silicon, so you can prove the post-silicon work actually exercised what pre-silicon left uncovered. Third, **assumption validation**: every CDC/RDC signoff, every formal proof, every emulation black box rests on assumptions, and those assumptions have to be re-checked once silicon arrives.

Why this matters is simple economics. Patel et al. point out that there is 'an increasing need of integrating/reusing the infrastructure across all the stages of product development right from pre-silicon design verification to post-silicon test validation, evaluation and applications board validation' [Unified Test Writing Framework for Pre and Post Silicon Verification, 2019]. Reinventing the testbench for each phase burns calendar time the program does not have, and breaks the traceability that lets you triage a lab bug back to a missed pre-silicon check.

## How it is used in practice

**Portable Stimulus (PSS)** has become the dominant medium for portable test content. Vasu, Venkatesh, and Maitra at Intel use PSS to carry media performance validation scenarios from emulation to post-silicon, observing that 'post silicon validation is very diverse, and the work starts well before the silicon is available in the lab' [Media Performance Validation in Emulation and Post Silicon Using Portable Stimulus Standard, 2021]. The latest expression of this pattern is the unified DV/SV/ATE environment from Suckert, Khaikin, and Vazhayil, which carries parameterized functional digital tests from pre-silicon DV all the way through silicon validation and onto ATE for high-volume manufacturing, explicitly targeting 'better Time-to-Quality (TTQ) while reducing Time-to-Market (TTM)' [Accelerating Device Sign-off through a Unified Environment for Design Verification, Silicon Validation, and ATE with PSS, 2025]. Patel's earlier unified framework made the same bet on UVM, exposing it through a layer that analog designers and test engineers could drive without knowing UVM internals [Unified Test Writing Framework for Pre and Post Silicon Verification, 2019].

For AMS designs, **test reuse** is even more valuable because each lab bring-up is expensive. Timmanagoudar et al. report a methodology that lets pre-silicon verification testcases flow directly into post-silicon functional validation, 'successfully implemented across many projects, verified and validated several products, with one day bring up of silicon' [Analog Mixed Signal Verification and Validation(V&V) Methodology, 2024]. Rutsch et al. use FPGA prototyping throughout the mixed-signal chip process — product definition, pre-silicon verification, post-silicon verification, customer design-in — collapsing 'regression runtime from roughly a month to hours' with an open-source MSDSL modeling flow [Boosting mixed-signal design productivity with FPGA-based methods throughout the chip design process, 2020].

For debug, **formal** has become the surprising post-silicon weapon. Hahlbeck and Upadhyay at NXP show that a single cover property is often sufficient to reproduce a post-silicon bug, taking 'advantage of the full state space exploration of formal tools' rather than rebuilding a simulation testbench [Unleash the Power of Formal for Post-Silicon Debugging, 2024]. Jain, Gupta, and KiranKumar present a stepwise guide for bringing formal into the post-silicon phase to reproduce failures in pre-silicon, validate bug fixes, and 'build a comprehensive solution to uncover additional vulnerabilities' [Never too late with formal, 2022]. Handover, Ayari, and Yeung even brand their post-silicon bug-hunting formal flow as 'deep sea fishing' — most bugs 'happen deep in functional operation under unusual combinations of events and scenarios' [Formal Verification Experiences: Silicon Bug Hunt with 'Deep Sea Fishing', 2020].

## Pitfalls and where the field is heading

The biggest pitfall is the **bug escape you cannot debug**. Deadlocks and livelocks are the canonical example: Jesrani argues that 'functional defects in these complex system designs that can result in such stall scenarios can increase exponentially' and 'are extremely challenging to debug or root cause in a post-silicon environment' — so they must be caught pre-silicon, with detection *and* localization, before tapeout [IDeALS for all -Intelligent Detection and Accurate Localization of Stalls, 2020]. The IDeALS flow localizes a stall back to a specific RTL block so the bug is fixable before it becomes a lab mystery.

The second pitfall is the **assumption gap**. Sinha and Christie are blunt: the assumptions used to sign off CDC and RDC 'have to be ensured mainly in the SoC design,' and an autonomous assertion-based flow has to validate them rather than letting them be waived [A step towards Zero Silicon Bugs using Assertion based Assumption Validation, 2020]. Reuse of test content has its own trap — Chin, Edwards, and Pfrimmer describe the work of taking validation content built for one environment and pushing it from IP to SoC, 'including headcount savings, RTL bugs found, and operational cost savings,' but only after the strategic decisions about scalability were made up front [Case Study: Successes and Challenges of Validation Content Reuse, 2022].

The field is converging on **digital twins and unified platforms**. Abd AlWahab et al. bridge pre- and post-silicon with a digital twin running the same stimulus across a virtual SoC platform, an FPGA prototype, and an NVIDIA Jetson edge device [Enhancing Automotive ECU Design with Digital Twin Simulation, 2026]. Subudhi et al. tackle UCIe multi-die PHY designs whose multi-module architecture has to verify identically across simulation, emulation, and silicon [Navigating the Maze: Verifying Multi-Module PHY designs in UCIe Multi-Die Systems, 2024]. The trend is unambiguous: the pre-silicon/post-silicon boundary is being erased, and the teams that win are the ones whose test content, coverage, and assumptions travel across it without loss.
