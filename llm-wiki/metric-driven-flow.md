# Metric-Driven Verification Flow

> *"Every project ends with the same question: are we done? For decades the answer was a vibe — engineers eyeballed logs, waved hands, and the project lead declared tapeout. Then metric-driven verification (MDV) arrived and replaced the vibe with a number. The bet is straightforward: take the architectural spec, decompose it into a hierarchy of features, attach a coverage measurement to each feature, run regressions, and let coverage — not opinion — drive the exit criterion. Khan, Kashai and Fang laid out the canonical definition back in 2011: 'The functional specification is parsed down to a hierarchy of features in a verification plan, where each feature can be shown to meet the specification by some measurement,' and those measurements are functional coverage. By 2021 the methodology was universal enough that Hristozkov, Pallister and Porter could open a paper titled 'No Country For Old Men' by noting MDV 'is universally accepted as the go-to methodology in verification.' Universal, but not finished — because the moment you wire coverage into your exit gate, every remaining argument becomes about how to collect, merge, trust, and act on the data. So what's actually flowing through an MDV pipeline, and why is the field still arguing about it?"

## What it is

An **MDV flow** is the end-to-end pipeline that connects the spec to a coverage number you can sign off on. The pipeline runs: **plan → features → coverage model → constrained-random regressions → results → dashboards**. Khan et al. describe the guiding principle crisply: 'MDV is guided by a functional specification, rather than design implementation,' and the spec-to-features decomposition is what turns an architectural intent into a measurable thing [Metric Driven Verification of Mixed-Signal Designs, 2011]. The reason this matters is that modern designs have 'state spaces so huge that only a tiny fraction of all possible combinations can be simulated,' and MDV is the discipline that extracts good functional coverage from that limiting circumstance.

The coverage model itself has two halves. The **functional coverage** half is what the engineer writes — covergroups and coverpoints encoding 'did we exercise the scenarios we said mattered?' The **code coverage** half (line, branch, toggle, FSM) is what the tool extracts from the implementation. Yang, Sha and Zhao, applying MDV to a mixed-signal ASSP at Analog Devices, argue that 'functional coverage is the main metric for measuring stimulus quality in a metric driven verification,' and that 'constraint random tests will usually cover more comprehensive verification points than traditional directed tests' [Metric Driven Mixed-Signal Verification Methodology and Practices for Complex Mixed-signal ASSPs, 2014]. Wang and Pusphaparaj make the same point and then expose the painful second half: 'to get coverage closure, large numbers of simulations tend to be required,' and merging that coverage data 'can take a significant amount of time and effort only to release the total coverage which may still not be 100%' [Practical experience in automatic functional coverage convergence, 2014].

## How it's used in practice

The interesting case studies are where MDV got **extended beyond digital-only SoCs**. Brownell and Schmitt document the migration of digital techniques — UVM, constrained random, MDV — into analog and mixed-signal blocks, with UVM-MS, real number modeling, and randomization now applied where directed tests used to rule [Digitizing Mixed Signal Verification, 2014]. Juneja, Prabhu and Veluri extend the same instinct to high-performance analog, noting that Real Valued Models 'provide, amidst other benefits, seamless integration with state-of-the-art digital verification methods such as MDV' [Practical Considerations for Real Valued Modeling of High Performance Analog Systems, 2016]. The MDV pitch turns out to be portable — wherever you can write a coverage model, you can run the pipeline.

The second extension is **closing the loop with automation**. Kodi, Patil and Nair at NVIDIA describe fully automated functional coverage closure, where 'coverage is analyzed, constraints updated, and tests rerun till the target is achieved' in a loop the engineer no longer babysits [Fully Automated Functional Coverage Closure, 2019]. Ega, Jeyapaul and Jani push the same automation into gate-level simulation, building a 'metric driven methodology to gauge the quality of GLS stimuli, which in turn boosts the Timing verification' [Leveraging more from GLS, 2018]. And Savić demonstrates that the framework reaches firmware too — an in-house tool built from SystemVerilog plus Perl/HTML/CSS/JS applies 'a metric-driven method for the firmware verification process that is easily reusable' in simulation and emulation [A Metric-driven Methodology For Firmware Verification, 2016].

The third, increasingly central extension is **plugging MDV into enterprise systems**. Graham's argument is that EDA-only metric integration is incomplete: 'these tools can help with the metrics that are directly generated by EDA, they often lack any meaningful integration with other Enterprise Applications such as defect tracking, requirements management, and source code changes' [Connecting Enterprise Applications to Metric Driven Verification, 2014]. Once you wire MDV to defect trackers and source control — appending the failing seed to the defect record — the coverage number stops being an island and starts driving the actual engineering workflow.

## Pitfalls and where the field is heading

The classic pitfall is that **SystemVerilog was never designed for data management at MDV scale**. Hristozkov et al. make this their central complaint: MDV 'has not kept up with the speed of innovation in exponentially more complex designs,' held back by 'the requirement to use SystemVerilog, a language not designed for large-scale data management,' and EDA-vendor flows are 'held back by the need to support legacy code' and 'drive users towards reliance on proprietary APIs' [No Country For Old Men, 2021]. Their response is an in-house Python implementation that frees the team from SystemVerilog's data-handling limits — coverage models defined in Python, results in SQL, dashboards in JavaScript. Rohleder, Röttgermann and Rüttiger make a parallel point for ISO 26262: MDV 'has suffered from the need to combine the results of different methods,' and recent developments lift those limits specifically for functional safety devices [Enhancements of metric driven verification for the ISO26262, 2016].

The deeper philosophical pitfall is that **functional coverage is itself subjective**. Chen and Ruhwandl argue that 'defining functional coverage itself is a subjective and dependent process largely based on human intellects,' which makes it hard to answer even 'whether the stimulated space is random enough.' They propose a 'Stimulus Domain' concept to explore the stimulation space along the time dimension systematically [Use Stimulus Domain for Systematic Exploration, 2018]. Baek, Hong and Jun take the data-driven answer further: heatmap visualization of verification gaps plus ML-driven regression optimization delivered 'a 63% reduction in regression testing time and improved SDC coverage to 100% for timing exception paths' at Samsung [Enhancing SDC Verification in SoCs, 2025].

The frontier is **AI closing the loop entirely**. Noh, Choi and Yim's Multi-Agent Orchestration reframes regression as 'a manager-centric flow' where 'failure and coverage signals are often siloed,' and proposes 'an AI-driven closed-loop regression architecture in which each run's outcomes can be fed back into the next' [Multi-Agent Orchestration for Autonomous Regression Management, 2026]. Where classical MDV ended with a dashboard a human had to read, the next-generation flow ends with an agent that reads it for you. The number is still the number; what changes is who turns it into the next decision.

- **Practical Considerations for Real Valued Modeling of High Performance Analog Systems** (2016, DVCon US) — Dushyant Juneja, Siddharth Prabhu and and Syam Veluri. Real Valued Models enabling seamless integration of analog verification with MDV.
