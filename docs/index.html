Q1 — Where are authors from? (papers & authors on a world map, over time)

Approach. For each paper we parse the affiliations_text block (a flat dump of author affiliation lines from GROBID + heuristic extraction) and recover the country of each author's institution via a curated alias map + city→country fallback. A paper can contribute to multiple countries (one count per distinct origin country mentioned), so international collaborations show up on both sides. Authors are deduplicated per year (case-folded, punctuation-stripped), so the same person counts once even if they appear on three papers that year.

Result. 76% of the 1,852 papers have at least one parseable origin country (1404 papers). The structured Affiliation.country column only covered 6% of the corpus and was inconsistent, so the flat text block is the trustworthy source. The top origin countries reflect the conference's geographic spread: India and the US dominate, with Germany, Korea and Taiwan forming the second tier.

Q2b — Programming / verification languages over time (racing bar)

Approach. Each paper's title + abstract + chunk text is scanned for mentions of 18 hardware/software languages and standards (SystemVerilog, Verilog, VHDL, Specman/e, SystemC, PSS, Python, C++, UVM/OVM/VMM, SVA, UPF, IP-XACT, TCL, Makefile). A paper is tagged with a language on first match. To avoid double-counting, "Verilog" is suppressed when "SystemVerilog" appears in the same paper (similarly "C" vs "SystemC"). A paper can be tagged with multiple languages.

Result. SystemVerilog is the most-mentioned language across the corpus (1122 papers). The racing bar shows how the verification-language ecosystem has shifted from Verilog/VHDL/Specman in the early 2010s to SystemVerilog/UVM dominance today, with Python and PSS rising sharply in recent years.

Caveat. "Mentioned" is not "used" -- a paper might cite UVM in passing while presenting a VHDL flow. Counts reflect presence of the language name, not adoption.

Q3 — Company contributions across time (industry vs academia, EDA vs startup vs large-cap)

Approach. Each affiliation line in a paper's affiliations_text is matched against a curated CSV of ~96 known EDA / semiconductor / automotive / academic entities (file: docs/data/companies.csv, hand-curated and editable). Each entry carries a year-dependent size bucket (large-cap / mid-cap / startup / academic). The classifier picks the bucket column whose year is closest to (but ≤) the paper's year, so e.g. AMD moves from mid-cap to large-cap across the 2017 Zen launch. Unmatched industry-looking fragments fall into Industry (unclassified) so the gap is visible.

Result. 32% of papers have at least one EDA vendor among the affiliations, confirming DVCon's identity as the industry-led verification conference. The size-class mix shows a steady large-cap dominance (Intel, NVIDIA, Samsung, Cadence, Synopsys, Siemens) with a long tail of small EDA consultancies and startups contributing methodology papers.

Refining. The companies CSV is intentionally checked in. To improve coverage, edit docs/data/companies.csv and re-run the generator; the unmatched "Industry (unclassified)" bar in chart 4 shows how many papers fall through.

Q5 — Reproducibility (% with open-source repo, ‘show cool but don’t teach’)

Approach. Each paper is scored 0–3 across six heuristic signals: github/gitlab/bitbucket repo link (in chunks or abstract), "open source" mention, "available at / artifact" mention, dataset/benchmark release, methodology markers (≥3 of Listing N / Algorithm N / Step N in the markdown), and explicit "reproducibility" mention. The composite score rewards both "teach how" (methodology listings) and "here's the code" (repo link). The signals are heuristic and noisy; a false-positive audit would require a manual sweep.

Result. Only 189 papers (10.2%) link to a public repo, and 356 (19.2%) mention "open source" at all. 108 papers (5.8%) have substantial methodology listings (a proxy for "teaches you how"). The "show-cool-but-don't-teach" bucket (score ≤1, no methodology, long abstract) captures 1272 papers (68.7% of the corpus). Reproducibility is materially lower than in ML conferences (where 30–50% link code), reflecting DVCon's industry-IP-sensitive culture.

Additional metrics worth tracking later: self-citation count (proxy for "builds on own prior art"), Flesch reading-ease of the abstract (proxy for "bothered to explain"), count of Figure N references (proxy for "showed diagrams"), count of Table N with comparison numbers (proxy for "ran benchmarks"). These are one-liners to add to the extractor; not computed here to avoid over-claiming.

Methodology & sources

Source. SQLite DB at data/dvcon.db (1,852 papers, 3,342 authors, 1,641 affiliations, 38,761 chunks). Read-only.

Origin extraction (Q1). Parse affiliations_text fragments, match country aliases (curated + pycountry), fall back to a city→country dict of ~30 entries.

Topic tagging (Q2). Hand-curated 16-topic taxonomy with regex keyword sets; multi-label match against title + abstract.

Company classification (Q3). Curated CSV of ~96 entities with year-dependent size buckets; heuristic fallback for unknown industry-looking strings; sentence-y fragments filtered.

Author analytics (Q4). Dedup via casefold + punctuation strip + honorific removal; use paper_author link table.

Reproducibility (Q5). Heuristic signals: github/gitlab/bitbucket regex, 'open source' / 'artifact' / 'dataset' mentions, ≥3 of Listing/Algorithm/Step markers in markdown. Composite 0–3 score.

Known gaps. Author-name collisions are first-normalized-wins. Company size buckets are approximate (founded_year + manual buckets at 5-year intervals). Reproducibility signals are presence-checks, not verification that the linked repo actually builds.
